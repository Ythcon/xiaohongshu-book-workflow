# Editorial and Layout Rules

## Choose the Book

When the user supplies only a designer's name, prefer a book with an explicit intellectual structure:

1. writing or manifesto by the designer;
2. interviews or conversations with sustained arguments;
3. archive, drawing, or process study that exposes method;
4. critical monograph organized around a thesis.

Avoid a generic complete-works album unless no theory-oriented book exists or the user asks for it. State why the chosen edition is useful to designers.

## Build One Argument

Compress the book into this chain:

```text
question → claim → mechanism → four tests → limit/nuance → reusable method
```

The claim must be contestable. “This book introduces the designer's works” is not a claim. A useful claim links an operation to an effect, such as “sequence turns climate into an architectural experience.”

Choose four pieces of evidence that do different jobs:

- origin or clearest demonstration;
- variation in another condition;
- tension, exception, or technical detail;
- consequence that returns to the thesis.

Do not use four similar glamour photographs as four cases.

## Six-Card Pacing

Keep one dominant sentence per card. Supporting copy should explain evidence rather than decorate it.

| Card | Editorial job | Visual priority |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | pose the question and identify the book | thesis + real cover |
| 02 | reveal the mechanism | strongest evidence |
| 03 | extend it | second evidence |
| 04 | complicate it | contrast/detail |
| 05 | complete the test | fourth evidence |
| 06 | synthesize | concept chain + methods |

## Design 01 From the Thesis

Choose a composition verb from the book: layer, sequence, frame, excavate, float, compress, collide, archive, or converse. Turn the verb into layout behavior. Examples:

- sequence: a staggered path, not a centered poster;
- archive: indexing, crops, annotations, and visible metadata;
- threshold: divided fields and a crossing element;
- conversation: two unequal text/image voices with a shared axis.

The real cover must remain identifiable. It need not always be centered, upright, or large.

## Design 06 From the Conclusion

Decide whether the conclusion is a loop, ladder, field, constellation, section, timeline, or set of tensions. Use that logic for the concept chain and three methods. Avoid a default grid of three equal boxes.

The closing card must answer card 01, not merely repeat its words. A useful method is actionable: “use a shaded transition to delay arrival,” not “pay attention to atmosphere.”

## End-Card Variation Check

For both 01 and 06, record:

- `layout_rationale` — why this structure belongs to this book;
- `changed_variables` — at least three major layout choices changed from the prior/default solution.

Fixed specifications are allowed. Fixed layouts are not.
